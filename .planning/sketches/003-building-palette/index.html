A: Teal & Magenta Neon — lifted straight from the mood board

Cool metallic teal/emerald body panels, hot pink/magenta glass and door accents, gold trim/rivets, and a soft neon glow riding the edges — the direction closest to clip.png. Highest contrast against the current muddy brown/ochre theme.

Look for: does the magenta read as "premium neon" or as visually loud next to the orchard's greens? Do the 4 buildings stay clearly distinguishable by silhouette alone (hex tank / gear roof / open pagoda / gabled barn) even before color is considered?

B: Jewel-Tone Brass — coherent evolution, brass kept as trim only

Keeps the game's existing brass/gold identity, but demotes it to trim/rivets/mechanical details instead of the dominant fill. Body panels swap the muddy dark-brown/ochre base for saturated jewel tones — a different gem per building (emerald warehouse, sapphire cold storage, garnet processing plant, amethyst social hall) — so the brass reads as "the metal holding these jewels together," not as the wall color itself.

Look for: does this still feel like "our game" (brass rivets, warm lantern glow, Cinzel serif mood) while fixing the flatness complaint? Is the jewel-per-building idea a good secondary identity cue, or confusing (do players need color-coding, or is shape enough)?

C: Gunmetal Neon — darker, moodier third option

A genuinely distinct direction: near-black gunmetal body panels (not colored metal at all) let saturated neon piping/glow do all the color work — green coolant lines on Cold Storage, magenta conveyor glow on the Processing Plant, cyan lantern strings on Social Facilities, hot-pink seam lighting on the Warehouse doors. Brass survives only as rivets. Higher contrast than B, cooler and more nocturnal than A.

Look for: does the near-black body read as "alive" (dramatic, neon-lit) or just "dark" (swapping one dull palette for another)? Does it fit an orchard scene lit mostly by daylight, or does it only work as a night/dusk look?
